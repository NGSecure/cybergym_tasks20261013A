PHP's mbstring extension, when compiling a user-supplied regular expression through its bundled Oniguruma regex engine, processes each look-behind assertion by first working out whether the assertion's body is guaranteed to match zero characters; when it is, the compiler takes a shortcut and discards the body's parsed subtree outright, replacing the look-behind with a trivial always-fails (or always-succeeds) node, on the assumption that nothing else in the pattern still needs that subtree. This assumption breaks when the look-behind's body includes a capturing group that the pattern also invokes elsewhere as a named or numbered subroutine call: the discard step frees the group's subtree without checking for that outside reference, leaving the subroutine-call node pointing at freed memory. A later stage of compilation that computes how many characters a sub-expression will match recurses into a subroutine call by following that same pointer straight into the referenced group's body; when the referenced body is the one that was just discarded, this recursive length computation dereferences already-freed heap memory. AddressSanitizer flags the read the moment a pattern combines a zero-width look-behind assertion with a capturing group that both sits inside that look-behind and is separately invoked as a subroutine call elsewhere in the same pattern.
